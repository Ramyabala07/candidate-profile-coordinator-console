Rebuilt for the Coordinator
01

Coordinator Console V8 · Candidate Profile

The panel is organised around time. The job isn't.

What's wrong with the candidate profile today, what we learned from products that solved this shape before us, and two ways to fix it.

Ramya Bala · Design · September 2026
02

The problem

A log answers “what happened”. A coordinator is asking something else.

What the panel is built to answer

What happened, and in what order?

  • Every event becomes a milestone on one rail, newest first
  • Perfect if you already know what you're looking for

What the coordinator actually asks

Where is this candidate, and why are they stuck?

  • A question about state, not sequence
  • The answer is scattered across the whole rail

The structure and the question are mismatched. That's why adding more cards kept making it worse — you can't fix a mismatch by adding to it.

03

What we have today

The panel as it stands

What you're looking at

  • A single reverse-chronological rail — every event becomes a milestone
  • Cards hang off the milestones: identity, payment, documents, checks
  • At its longest today it runs roughly four screens
  • It grows with every event the candidate generates
  • It reads as a story of what happened, in the order it happened
04

Audit · across all sixteen finalised variants

What to keep, what to replace

Keep — this part is good

  • The dot colours. A real four-value taxonomy, and the red dot is the only thing readable at a glance.
  • The card grammar. Absorbs photos, amounts and key-values without breaking.
  • “Unable to match” vs “Mismatch”. Bad input versus a failed check — a distinction most products miss entirely.
  • The inline name diff. Explains a failure in two lines, with no prose.

Replace

  • There is no summary. The panel opens straight into the log — nothing states the condition of the enrollment before you start reading.
  • Everything carries the same weight. “Invited” and “Tab switch detected” are typographically identical.
  • The verdict is never stated. Identity appears in five places and is resolved in none — the coordinator reconciles it themselves.
  • Re-enrollment has nowhere to live. One rail can't hold a journey that repeats.
05

What we designed for

One job

When a candidate is stuck, flagged, or disputing an outcome, show me what happened and why — so I can make a decision I can defend.

Principle 01

Verdict before evidence

  • The answer first. The working underneath, for whoever needs it.

Principle 02

Evidence reachable, not resident

  • Photos and scans one level down — never inline by default.

Principle 03

Status is derived, not declared

  • Worked out from the stages — so the header names the blocker, not the last event.
06

The honest version — not “less scrolling”

Both designs scroll. Only one of them has a ceiling.

Today — length follows the candidate

  • Every event appends a milestone, so activity sets the length
  • The activity log adds sixteen more event types; re-enrolment replays the whole journey again
  • Two candidates on the same workflow can differ tenfold

Grows without limit

Proposed — length follows the workflow

  • One row per stage the workflow actually configures
  • Activity moves to its own tab — depth on demand, not by default
  • Two candidates on the same workflow look the same length

Nine rows, whatever happens

07

The test that matters

What can you answer without scrolling at all?

Today

  • The newest event — not the most important one.
  • On a finished exam that's “Certification issued”. It confirms how the story ended, but says nothing about whether anything went wrong on the way.
  • Nothing about a pending accommodation, a failed payment, or a flagged identity
  • To find out why someone is stuck, you read the whole panel

Proposed

  • A status worked out from the stages, so it names the actual blocker
  • Everything waiting on you, shown at the stage it belongs to
  • Every stage with its own verdict — nine rows
  • Scrolling becomes opt-in: you scroll into evidence you chose to open

12

The change, concretely

Same content. Different architecture.

Today

  • A chronological rail — the log is the structure
  • Opens on the last thing that happened
  • No statement of condition anywhere
  • Evidence inline at full weight

Proposed

  • A stage ladder — the enrollment's own logic is the structure
  • Opens on what needs you
  • Status worked out from the stages, naming the blocker
  • One verdict per stage, evidence one level down
